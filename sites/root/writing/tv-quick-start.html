A TV that took a minute and a half to turn on.

Part 10 · the screen itself

2026-11-09 home lab series · part 10 of 10

Part 9 put the lab's status on the living-room TV. The TV itself was the slow part. It is a 2017 Vizio M-Series (M50-E1) running Vizio's SmartCast software. Turning it on took between one and one and a half minutes, and switching between the Apple TV and the lab machine dragged. Once the Apple TV was up, everything was fine. The TV had been factory-reset recently, so years of leftover apps were not the cause.

I had seen videos of people wiping a smart TV and flashing a lighter system onto it, and that was the first idea. It ended as one setting changed through the TV's own API, plus two new keys on the dashboard.

NO FIRMWARE TO FLASH

The flashing videos are mostly about LG's webOS, which has an active rooting community, or about plugging a small computer into a spare HDMI port. For Vizio's SmartCast I found nothing comparable: no custom firmware, and no way in short of opening the case and probing the board. Updates arrive only through Vizio's own signed mechanism. Forcing something else onto the TV risks a dead TV, with nothing better to run on it anyway.

It does not need a new system, though. The Apple TV and the lab machine already do the "smart" part. The goal shifted to making the TV behave like a screen: come on fast, go to the right input, and do nothing else in the background.

THE TV HAS AN API

SmartCast TVs answer HTTPS on port 7345: the API that Vizio's phone app uses. It has a self-signed certificate, hence -k. One endpoint needs no login and reports the model and firmware. That is how this turned out to be an M-Series, not the P-Series I had assumed:

FROM A LAPTOP ON THE LAN: WHAT IS THIS TV?

dns-sd -B _viziocast._tcp local                 # find it (macOS); avahi-browse on Linux
curl -sk https://192.168.1.50:7345/state/device/deviceinfo \
  | jq '.ITEMS[0].VALUE | {MODEL_NAME, SERIES: .SYSTEM_INFO.SERIES, VERSION: .SYSTEM_INFO.VERSION}'

Settings need a token. You get one by pairing, the same way the phone app does: ask to pair, the TV shows a four-digit PIN, and you send the PIN back.

PAIR ONCE: THE PIN APPEARS ON THE TV

curl -sk -X PUT -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"DEVICE_ID":"lab-ctl-01","DEVICE_NAME":"Lab Controller"}' \
  https://192.168.1.50:7345/pairing/start          # returns PAIRING_REQ_TOKEN

curl -sk --max-time 90 -X PUT -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"DEVICE_ID":"lab-ctl-01","CHALLENGE_TYPE":1,"RESPONSE_VALUE":"<PIN>","PAIRING_REQ_TOKEN":<n>}' \
  https://192.168.1.50:7345/pairing/pair           # returns AUTH_TOKEN; keep it out of any repo

The --max-time 90 is there because the first attempt used 20 seconds. The TV accepted the PIN after curl had given up, so the token never arrived. A retry with the same PIN was rejected, because the PIN was already used, and the pairing had to start over with a new one.

WHERE THE MINUTE WENT

The whole settings menu is a tree under /menu_native/dynamic/tv_settings. A small script that walks it with GET requests only printed every setting. Changing nothing was deliberate, because I wanted the full picture first. One line explained the wait: 

/system/power_mode            Power Mode            Eco Mode
/timers/auto_power_off_timer  Auto Power Off        10 minutes
/system/reset_and_admin/smart_interactivity  Viewing Data   On
/system/reset_and_admin/advertising/ad_replacement  Ad Replacement  On

In Eco Mode, "off" really is off, so every power-on is a full boot of the TV's Linux system and its app stack. Auto Power Off makes it worse: ten minutes without a signal, say after the Apple TV goes to sleep, and the TV shuts down completely again.

The probing caught the TV in a bad state too. At one point its API accepted connections, completed the TLS handshake and then never answered, for minutes. A soft power cycle (System → Reset & Admin) cleared it. After that restart, the API took about half a minute after the Vizio logo to come up. Two other settings also stood out: Viewing Data, which identifies what is on screen and reports it back, and Ad Replacement. That is background work on a 2017 chip, for features nobody in the house wanted.

THE FIX

Every setting carries a HASHVAL. To change one, you read it and send the hash back with the new value; the allowed values are under the static half of the same tree:

READ THE OPTIONS, THEN CHANGE ONE

TV=https://192.168.1.50:7345/menu_native
curl -sk -H "AUTH: $TOKEN" $TV/static/tv_settings/system/power_mode | jq -c '.ITEMS[0].ELEMENTS'
# ["Eco Mode","Quick Start"]

HASH=$(curl -sk -H "AUTH: $TOKEN" $TV/dynamic/tv_settings/system/power_mode | jq '.ITEMS[0].HASHVAL')
curl -sk -X PUT -H "AUTH: $TOKEN" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d "{\"REQUEST\":\"MODIFY\",\"HASHVAL\":$HASH,\"VALUE\":\"Quick Start\"}" \
  $TV/dynamic/tv_settings/system/power_mode       # {"STATUS":{"RESULT":"SUCCESS"}}

Four changes, each read back afterwards:

SETTINGBEFOREAFTER
Power ModeEco ModeQuick Start
Viewing DataOnOff
Ad ReplacementOnOff
Limited Ad TrackingOffOn

Auto Power Off stayed at ten minutes. Under Quick Start the TV drops into a warm standby instead of shutting down, so waking from it is fast. The trade is a little more power drawn while the TV looks off.

TWO KEYS ON THE DASHBOARD

With a token in hand, switching inputs is the same read-then-write on devices/current_input. towerview, the dashboard from part 9, now has two keys: a puts the TV on the Apple TV and p on the lab machine. They work from the keyboard at the machine or from a laptop over SSH. The footer shows which input the TV is on, and for a few seconds after a key press, whether it worked: 

  s root shell     r redraw     a tv: apple tv     p tv: arttower     ctrl+alt+f2 root login       tv on apple tv

Three details. The call runs on its own thread, so a slow TV cannot freeze the screen. The TV's certificate is self-signed, which the Rust HTTP client refuses without a custom verifier, so towerview calls curl -k. And the token goes to curl on standard input (-H @-) rather than on the command line, where any user on the host could read it in ps. The token itself lives in a root-owned file that only the dashboard's user can read.

WHAT IS LEFT

A fixed address for the TV on the router, so the keys keep working if it ever gets a new one. Beyond that, the TV is now what it was always meant to be here: a screen that comes on when asked.

ASK AN AGENT

PROMPT: MY SMART TV IS SLOW TO TURN ON

My smart TV takes over a minute to turn on and is slow to switch inputs. It is on my home network. Find out why with read-only checks first, and change nothing until I agree.

- Find the TV on the network (mDNS, the router's client list) and identify the exact model and firmware.
- Check whether it has a local API (Vizio SmartCast on 7345, LG, Samsung, Roku and Android TV all have one) and what it needs to pair.
- Read the power mode, auto power-off, input-on-power-up, CEC and viewing-data or ad settings.
- Tell me honestly whether custom firmware exists for this model, and what flashing would risk.
Then propose the smallest changes, with how to undo each one.
